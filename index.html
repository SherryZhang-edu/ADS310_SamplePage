The Global Decline of Fertility Rates

This short teaching page summarizes selected information from Our World in Data. Your task is to transform this information into a structured LaTeX mini report.

Topic Overview

Fertility rates have declined substantially across the world over the last several decades. In the past, many families had a larger number of children. Today, in many countries and regions, the average number of children per woman is much lower.

This long-term change is connected to several social and economic developments, including women's education and employment opportunities, lower child mortality, higher costs of raising children, and improved access to family planning.

Guiding Question

How has the global fertility rate changed over time, and what social factors may help explain this decline?

Key Concept: Total Fertility Rate

The total fertility rate, or TFR, is commonly used to summarize fertility in a particular year. It estimates the average number of children that would be born to a hypothetical woman if she experienced the age-specific fertility rates observed in that year throughout her reproductive life.

In other words, TFR is a period measure. It describes fertility conditions in a specific year rather than tracking the actual lifetime fertility of one generation of women.

Definition

Total Fertility Rate = average expected number of children per woman, based on fertility rates in a particular year.

Short Discussion Material

Possible Reasons for Fertility Decline

Women's Education and Empowerment

When women have greater access to education and employment, they often have more life choices. The opportunity cost of having many children may also increase.

Declining Child Mortality

When child mortality is high, families may have more children to ensure that some survive. As child survival improves, families may choose to have fewer children.

Higher Investment in Each Child

As education and living costs rise, families may place more emphasis on investing resources in each child rather than having a larger number of children.

Family Planning

Access to family planning and contraception can help families make decisions about the number and timing of children.
